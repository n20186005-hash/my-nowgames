More Than Just a Game: How Pips Game Became a Teacher’s Secret Weapon for Mental Charging and Classroom Teaching

January 10, 2026 Middle School Math Teacher

As a middle school math teacher, my daily work is dealing with various formulas and exercises, and I also need to pay attention to the students' learning status at all times, which consumes a lot of mental energy. Before, to relieve work pressure, I started playing NYT's mini crosswords and Sudoku games. Every day, I play a round using 10 minutes between classes or during lunch breaks, which can not only relax my brain, but also exercise logical thinking, making me more energetic in subsequent teaching. But NYT's games have a big problem: some levels are too difficult, and you need to pay to unlock more content. For someone like me who just wants daily "mental charging", the cost performance is not high.

The Search for an Accessible Brain Teaser

Later, in a teacher exchange group, a colleague recommended Pips Game, saying that it is a "substitute for NYT Games and completely free". I downloaded it with a try attitude. After opening it, I found that its interface design is very simple, no unnecessary ads interfere, and the game rules are very simple, easy to get started. After playing the first round, I found that this game is particularly suitable for "mental charging"—each game takes about 10 minutes, the difficulty is moderate, which can make people concentrate, and will not produce a sense of frustration because it is too difficult.

Teacher-Tested Strategies: Edges and Frequency

After playing for a period of time, I summed up a few small skills suitable for quick clearance. The first one is "fix the edge first, then attack the center". The rows and columns on the edge often have fewer numbers, so it is easier to determine the position of the dominoes. For example, the top row of the board has only four numbers, which can just place two dominoes, and the combination can be quickly determined through the elimination method; putting the edge dominoes first can form a relatively fixed framework, and then deducing the domino combination in the center area will not easily go wrong.

The second skill is "pay attention to repeated points". If a certain point appears many times on the board, such as "3" appearing six times, then the corresponding "3-3" domino must exist, and its position can be quickly found through counting. For example, if there are three "3"s in a column, there is a high probability that there is a "3-3" domino in this column, and the remaining "3" is combined with the adjacent number, which can quickly advance the problem-solving progress.

Gamifying the Classroom

What surprises me is that the problem-solving ideas of Pips Game can also be used in teaching. Once, when I was explaining knowledge points related to "logical reasoning", I found that many students did not have a thorough understanding of "elimination method" and "hypothesis method". At this time, I suddenly thought of the problem-solving process of Pips Game, so I moved the game to the classroom. By demonstrating how to solve domino puzzles with elimination method and hypothesis method, students can understand these logical methods more intuitively. Unexpectedly, the effect was particularly good. The originally obscure knowledge points were quickly mastered by the students through the game demonstration. Some students even took the initiative to ask me the name of this game after class, saying that they wanted to download it to play to exercise logical thinking.

Now I insist on playing a round of Pips Game every day. Whether it's after arriving at school in the morning, during breaks between classes, or after school in the afternoon, as long as I have 10 minutes of free time, I will open it and play for a while. We will share our problem-solving skills with each other, and even discuss how to integrate the logical thinking methods in the game into the teaching of different subjects. For example, Chinese reading comprehension and English cloze test can use similar logical reasoning ideas.
