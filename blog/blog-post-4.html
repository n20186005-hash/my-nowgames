From Connections to Pips Game: A Math Student's Logic Puzzle Addiction

January 9, 2026 Math Major & Puzzle Fan

As a college student majoring in mathematics, I have no resistance to all games that require logical reasoning. I was recommended NYT's Connections by my classmates before. The feeling of finding related categories from a bunch of seemingly irrelevant words is simply addictive. Every night after self-study, I will play a few rounds with my roommates to compete who can clear the level faster. But the update speed of Connections' question bank is relatively slow, and sometimes when encountering some words that are biased towards American culture, it is difficult for us Chinese students to understand. After playing for a long time, we will feel a bit aesthetic fatigue.

A Mathematical Twist on Logic Puzzles

Until last month, I saw someone recommending Pips Game on a math enthusiast forum, saying that it is a "domino puzzle that plays tricks with mathematical constraints" and also mentioned that the gameplay is as addictive as Connections. I immediately became interested. After downloading and opening it, I found that the core of this game is indeed similar to Connections—both solve problems by finding "connections". The only difference is that Connections finds word connections, while Pips Game finds connections between numbers and dominoes. This familiar sense of "finding rules" made me have a sense of substitution immediately.

Parity and Reverse Deduction: My Strategies

When I first started playing, I quickly mastered the basic gameplay relying on my mathematical sensitivity. But to clear the level quickly, some skills are still needed. The first skill I summed up is "using mathematical parity to judge". The point combination of dominoes is either two even numbers, two odd numbers, or one odd and one even number. The total number of digits in each row and column is fixed. By judging the parity of the numbers in the rows and columns, you can quickly eliminate many impossible domino combinations. For example, if the sum of the numbers in a row is odd, then there must be an odd number of dominoes with "one odd and one even" in the domino combinations of this row, which can narrow down the selection range.

Another skill is "reverse deduction", which is especially useful when encountering complex levels. For example, when playing a 10x10 master-level level, positive deduction is easy to fall into chaos. At this time, I will first see which dominoes have not been used, and then reverse judge where they may appear on the board according to the points of the unused dominoes. For example, if there are still three dominoes left: "0-6", "1-5", and "2-4", and a certain column on the board is exactly missing these six numbers, then I can first lock the positions of these three dominoes in this column, and then adjust according to the numbers in the adjacent rows.

Where Hobbies Meet Academics

What I like most about Pips Game is that it makes mathematical constraints particularly interesting. As a student majoring in mathematics, I usually come into contact with boring formulas and theorems, while Pips Game integrates mathematical knowledge such as parity and number combinations into the game, allowing me to imperceptibly consolidate professional knowledge while playing the game. Once in our professional course, we talked about "combinatorial mathematics". The example given by the teacher was actually consistent with my problem-solving idea of playing Pips Game. I immediately raised my hand to share my game skills and was praised by the teacher. The feeling of combining hobbies and majors is really wonderful.

A New Dormitory Ritual

Now Pips Game has become a new "evening activity" for my roommates and me. We are no longer limited to playing together, but also ask each other questions——for example, I use the custom function in the game to generate a level for my roommate to solve, then I observe his problem-solving ideas next to him, and share my skills when he gets stuck, and vice versa. This interaction not only makes the game more fun, but also allows us to learn a lot of new problem-solving methods from each other. A roommate who was not interested in mathematics before also started playing Pips Game under my influence. Now he even says "so mathematics can be so fun" and even takes the initiative to ask me some basic mathematical knowledge. This is probably the charm of the game.

Perfect for Student Life

For students, another great advantage of Pips Game is that it is completely free and has no ads. We usually have limited living expenses and are reluctant to pay for game subscriptions. But Pips Game is not only free, but also can generate unlimited new questions, so we no longer have to worry about "question shortage". Sometimes 10 minutes before class, I will also open it and play a round, which can not only relax my mood, but also activate my brain, preparing for the next class.

I also found that the difficulty gradient of Pips Game is set very reasonably. From the 4x4 introductory level to the 10x10 master-level level, beginners can improve step by step, and will not be discouraged by encountering too difficult levels at the beginning. I can now easily clear the 8x8 level and am rushing towards the 10x10 level. Every time I conquer a high-difficulty level, I will take a screenshot and post it on Moments, getting a lot of likes. That sense of accomplishment is really full.
