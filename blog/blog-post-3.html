From NYT to Pips Game: A White-Collar Worker's Ultimate Decompression Tool

January 8, 2026 Office Puzzle Enthusiast

As an internet white-collar worker who deals with a lot of data and plans every day, my brain is like a high-speed running machine. During the day, it's filled with meetings and reports. When I get home from work at night, I always want to find something that can make my thinking "slow down" without being boring to relax. I used to follow the trend and play NYT's Wordle and Connections, which were really addictive. Especially the sense of ritual of opening the app to unlock new questions every morning has become a small motivation for me to start work. But good times don't last long. After using up NYT's free quota, advanced levels require a paid subscription. For someone like me who just wants to decompress daily, the monthly subscription fee of tens of yuan always seems unnecessary. But I couldn't find a replacement puzzle game. During that time, I felt that something was missing every lunch break.

Finding the Perfect Replacement

Until last month, I caught a glimpse of my colleague playing Pips Game at her desk. I was attracted by its simple interface at first sight—no gaudy ads, only a clear domino board and number prompts, which instantly gave me the feeling of NYT. My colleague said the game is completely free and can generate unlimited new questions. I scanned the code and downloaded it on the spot. I didn't expect to be completely hooked after playing it once.

From Novice to Strategist

When I first started playing, I chose the simplest 4x4 board. I originally thought that dominoes were just matching numbers, but I got stuck after placing just two dominoes. Either the number of digits in a row didn't match, or I accidentally used a repeated domino. Later, I slowly summed up a skill: first find "isolated numbers" and "paired numbers". For example, if there is only one "3" in a row on the board, this "3" must form a domino with the number in the adjacent grid. This is "breaking the game with isolated numbers"; if there are two "5"s in a column and their positions are adjacent, it is likely to be the "5-5" domino. Putting such certain combinations first makes the subsequent problem-solving smoother.

A Daily Ritual for Decompression

Now, every morning when I arrive at the company, I will first play a round of Pips Game as a "mental warm-up" to start the day. After work in the evening, I will play another round on the subway to decompress. When encountering complex 6x8 boards, I also use the "elimination method + marking method"—list the dominoes I have used in my mind, and mark the uncertain combinations first. For example, if a grid may be "2-4" or "2-6", I will first note down both possibilities, and then eliminate them according to the numbers in other rows and columns. Once I was playing an 8x8 level on the subway, I got so engrossed that I almost missed my stop. Fortunately, the game has a pause function. I quickly paused, got off the subway, found a bench by the road and continued playing. When I cleared the level, I felt refreshed and all the fatigue of the day was gone.

Unlimited Puzzles, Enhanced Logic

What surprises me most is its "unlimited" setting. I no longer have to wait for daily updates like NYT, nor do I have to worry about running out of free quota. Sometimes when I have nothing to do at home on weekends, I can play for more than an hour in a row, from 4x4 to 10x10 master-level levels. Every time I clear a high-difficulty level, the sense of accomplishment is stronger than completing a work project. Moreover, after playing for a long time, I found that my logic in dealing with work problems has become clearer. I used to be messy when sorting out complex reports, but now I will subconsciously sort out the core data first (corresponding to the problem-solving breakthrough) like solving domino puzzles, and then gradually sort out the associated data (corresponding to matching dominoes), which has greatly improved my efficiency.

Sparking Office Connections

I also dragged several colleagues in the same group to play together. We set up a small group and share our clearance screenshots and problem-solving skills every day. A colleague who didn't play puzzle games much before also fell in love with Pips Game under my influence. She said that she no longer scrolls short videos during lunch breaks. Playing a round of domino puzzles makes her more energetic in work in the afternoon. We also hold regular "level-breaking competitions". Whoever can clear the specified difficulty level in the shortest time will buy milk tea. This sense of interaction is something I didn't experience when I played NYT before.

The Ideal Decompression Tool

For white-collar workers like us who need a lot of mental output every day, Pips Game is really a treasure game. Unlike other games that require a lot of time and money investment, you can get a full sense of accomplishment in 10 minutes a day, and you can also exercise logical thinking. The key is that it is completely free and ad-free. Now it has become an indispensable app on my phone. Whether it's commuting, lunch break or slacking off, I can open it and play a round at any time to relax and activate my tense brain.
